The short version

Draft

Work is changing, not ending. As AI takes on more of the routine work, human capability becomes more important, not less, and leaders have more agency than they think. The Frontier Mindset Lab helps organizations redesign work around both what AI can do and what people can do, and builds the human skills and mindsets that make that possible.

The moment we're in

Draft

The conversation about AI and work is one of the most important of our time, and right now it is anchored around fear.

Two beliefs drive that fear:

  1. BeliefFatalism

    the sense that this moment is pre-determined, and that AI is in control, not us.

  2. BeliefA fixed mindset about human capability

    the idea that AI can keep getting better, but we can't.

Both are understandable. Neither is helpful, or true.

Business leaders have moved beyond asking whether to adopt AI. They are asking how to bring their workforce along and redesign their organization so AI empowers people to learn, adapt and create value in new ways.

What we believe

Draft

From Open to Work

  1. Work is changing, not ending.

    Jobs change, some go and new ones come, as in past disruptions to work.

  2. Human capability is becoming more important, not less.

    AI takes on the standardized, routine work, which opens room for more of our uniquely human capabilities.

    Story for this idea: The House and the Door

  3. We all have more agency than we think.

    Nothing about this moment is pre-determined. How technology changes work is shaped by human choices.

    Story for this idea: The House and the Door

From anxiety to adaptation, agency, aspiration and creation. Think of it as a pro-human, pro-AI playbook for what's next for work.

From anxiety to creation

Working framework

The Lab sees two mindset shifts. The human skills everyone talks about one by one live inside these two.

The mindset to adapt.

Think of an athlete working with a coach: continually adjusting as the game changes.

The mindset to create.

The entrepreneurial mindset from Open to Work: "doing more than is reasonable with the resources you have."

  2. 2Adaptationadaptation, not simply adoption
  3. 3Agencypeople see they have more choice than they think

What it means

Draft

What it means for workers

  • Jobs are tasks, not titles.
  • Careers are climbing walls, not ladders.
  • Nobody beats you at being you.

What it means for companies

Four focus areas for companies

  1. Focus on mission and culture
  2. Focus on behavior change for individuals and teams
  3. Focus on new workflows and projects that are AI capability-enabled
  4. Focus on new work and impact that is human capability-enabled

Source: What's Next for Work? presentation by Aneesh Raman

  1. Org design may be the most important thing a leadership team builds this decade.

    The org chart is now the starting point for managing change, not the end point. Look at workflows and cross-functional projects.

Old era

the Industrial Age.

The machine at the center of work.

Managed through the org chart.

New era

the AI Age.

The mind at the center of work.

Managed through the work chart.

Leaders now have to manage both charts.

Source: What's Next for Work? presentation by Aneesh Raman

  1. This is multi-dimensional change.

    It is happening top-down, middle-out and bottom-up at once, over months, not years. Leaders set the vision and culture (pro-human and pro-AI), managers coach and back new thinking, and individuals reshape their own jobs and workflows.

  2. Adoption needs behavior change.

    Asking for adoption without supporting behavior change doesn't work.

  3. Ask what AI is for.

    Much of the conversation is about what AI can do. The design question is what AI is for. We are entering an age of differentiation, where the old era's standardization and stability give way to differentiation and adaptability.

Building human capability

Draft

  • Curiosity

    asking "what if?" and exploring beyond what you know

  • Courage

    acting in the face of uncertainty and risk

  • Communication

    sharing ideas clearly and listening deeply

  • Creativity

    connecting ideas to make something new

  • Compassion

    understanding and caring about others' needs

  • Resilience and adaptability

    bouncing back from setbacks and adjusting as conditions change

How to build it

  1. Build new mindsets through deliberate practice

  2. Start with your own calendars and meetings

  3. Shift manager focus from tasks to people

  4. Define and discuss human skills, not just technical skills

Microsoft and the Lab

Microsoft's role

Draft

Microsoft has five decades of experience helping companies manage changes to work, and is a leader in enterprise AI, helping companies build AI tools and agents safely and securely.

What is AI for? Microsoft's answer is our mission: to empower every person and every organization on the planet to achieve more.

Customer Zero

Microsoft applies this to itself first:

  1. Start with the business outcome, not the technology
  2. Redesign the entire workflow, not just individual tasks
  3. Put employees at the center of transformation
  4. Use AI to expand what people can do
  5. Combine human and AI capability to create a continuously learning organization

Questions the Lab is exploring

Working framework

We're a lab. These are the questions we're working to answer, and we'll share what we learn, including what doesn't work.

  1. What's stopping people from adapting to this new era and, as a first step, adopting AI?
  2. What distinguishes workers who are adapting from those who aren't?
  3. Which human skills become more important as people work more deeply, and well, with AI tools and agents?
  4. How can AI itself best help people develop these mindsets?
  5. What new work, roles and business growth emerge when these mindsets and AI use come together?

How it was made
  1. Start with a recording: a 1:58 talk from AfroTech.

Say it to leaders

For business leaders

Where are the doors in your organization?

Inside a company, the doors are degree requirements, title-gated promotion and closed internal mobility. Leaders can open them by hiring, promoting and paying for human skills.

Draft

Discovery questions

  • Which roles still require a degree that the work doesn't?
  • How do people move into higher-value roles today, and who gets left outside?
  • Which human skills do your best people show that your systems don't measure?
  • Where could AI remove a gate rather than add one?
  • What would a strengths-first talent process look like here?

    About The Frontier Mindset Lab

    Mission

    Draft

    Our North Star

    Redesign work around both what AI can do and what people can do, and ultimately help redesign the labor market itself, so that human skills and behavioral mindsets show up in how people access economic opportunity and are hired, promoted and paid.

    The short version: Work is changing, not ending. As AI takes on more of the routine work, human capability becomes more important, not less, and leaders have more agency than they think. The Frontier Mindset Lab helps organizations redesign work around both what AI can do and what people can do, and builds the human skills and mindsets that make that possible.
